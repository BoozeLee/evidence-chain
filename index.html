House rules

Editor's note

Let's get one thing straight before anybody gets a hard-on for a conspiracy. This magazine hates two things equally. The first is power that hides its paperwork. The second is the lazy bastard on the internet who draws a red line between two famous names, adds a skull emoji, and calls it journalism.

Both of them are the same disease. Both treat a feeling as a fact. Powerful institutions say "trust us" and want you to stop asking. Network-diagram merchants say "it's obvious" and want you to stop checking. We're not doing either one.

So here's the deal. Every claim in this edition carries a grade, taken from the Project Evidence Chain dossiers it's built on. If a claim has a primary document behind it, you'll see it. If it doesn't, you'll see that too, in red, at the end of every chapter. We never round a C up to an A because it would make a better headline.

ADocumented. Primary source or strong independent confirmation. Can be stated as fact.
BPlausible, unconfirmed. The mechanism is possible; the decisive proof is missing.
CSpeculative. A possible explanation without enough evidence. Never published as an accusation.
DContradicted. Strong available evidence says otherwise.
EFiction or symbol. Culturally meaningful, proves nothing that happened.

One rule sits above the scale: association is not causation, timing is not proof, and symbolism is not evidence. Two people in the same building aren't in the same conspiracy. A date that lines up is a coincidence until a document says otherwise.

We will not invent the missing link. We'll point at the hole and tell you exactly what would fill it.

That isn't cowardice. It's the only thing that makes the real findings bulletproof. The documented part of this story is ugly enough on its own: a man paid to open doors for the world's largest asset manager walked through a lot of government doors, and the system was built so nobody had to write down what was said. Inflate that with garbage and you hand every PR department in Berlin a free way to dismiss all of it.

01

The supervisory chair

What Merz actually did at BlackRock, 2016 to 2020

In March 2016, Friedrich Merz, the former CDU parliamentary leader whom Angela Merkel had pushed out of front-line politics, landed a new gig. BlackRock, the asset manager with more money under management than most countries have in GDP, made him chairman of the supervisory board of BlackRock Asset Management Deutschland AG, its German legal entity. M-01 · A

That much is boring corporate fact. The interesting part is the second half of the announcement. BlackRock said Merz would also take a broader advisory role, building relationships with major clients, regulators and German government bodies. M-02 · A Manager Magazin read that and called him what he plainly was hired to be: a lobbyist. "Lobbyist" wasn't his legal title. But when a company tells you a former top politician's job is to cultivate regulators and government, you don't need a law degree to get the point.

This is a man who, in November 2018, while running for the CDU leadership, told Bild he would count himself among the "upper middle class," then told Bild am Sonntag he earned around a million euros a year before tax. That should tell you how comfortable he was with the gap between the job title and the job.

What the chair is, and isn't

Now for the part the internet always skips. A German Aktiengesellschaft has a two-tier structure. The management board runs the company. The supervisory board watches the management board. The chair of the supervisory board doesn't place trades, doesn't run portfolios, and doesn't pick stocks for funds. In the same announcement, BlackRock said Harald Klug, its COO for Germany, Austria and Eastern Europe, would run day-to-day business in the region. M-03 · D

So every meme that says "Merz was CEO of BlackRock" is wrong. He wasn't CEO of BlackRock Inc., and he wasn't CEO of BlackRock Germany. M-04 · D Repeating that line doesn't make you a truth-teller. It makes you the easiest person in the room to discredit.

And the role wasn't just a title either. Der Spiegel reported that he took part in a conference call for about 800 BlackRock clients alongside former UK chancellor George Osborne. M-05 · A He did the job. The job was access.

He stayed on the board even during his 2018 run for the CDU leadership, which he lost to Annegret Kramp-Karrenbauer, and left the German chairmanship at the end of March 2020. Remember that date. People will try to make it carry a lot of weight.

This proves

  • Merz chaired the supervisory board of BlackRock's German entity from March 2016 to March 2020.
  • His brief explicitly included relationships with clients, regulators and government.
  • He actively worked client and network events.

This does not prove

  • That he ran BlackRock, in Germany or anywhere.
  • That he picked any investment, in pharma or anything else.
  • That his relationships changed any government decision.

Remaining gap: no certified Handelsregister extract, articles of association or board minutes for 2016–2020 have been retrieved. The role is strongly confirmed by contemporary sources, but his exact statutory powers are still unreconstructed.

02

The money that was real

Where the vaccine money actually came from

If you want to know who paid for the Pfizer–BioNTech vaccine, you don't need a whistleblower. You need a press release, a bank statement and some patience. The money trail is public, and it doesn't run through BlackRock.

  1. March 2016Merz becomes supervisory board chair of BlackRock Asset Management Deutschland AG.
  2. 17 Oct 2017 – 12 Sep 2018Four reported meetings with senior federal officials in his BlackRock capacity (chapter 04).
  3. 17 March 2020Pfizer and BioNTech announce their COVID-19 vaccine collaboration.
  4. Late March 2020Merz leaves the BlackRock Germany chairmanship.
  5. 9 April 2020Pfizer publishes the financial terms.
  6. April 2020Germany's Paul-Ehrlich-Institut authorizes BioNTech's first German trial. Its FAQ says 20 April; its public announcement is dated 22 April.
  7. 11 June 2020The European Investment Bank signs up to €100 million in debt financing with BioNTech.
  8. July 2020The large phase 2/3 trial is under way.
  9. 15 September 2020BioNTech announces up to €375 million from Germany's research ministry (BMBF).
  10. 21 December 2020The EMA recommends conditional marketing authorization for Comirnaty.
  11. 6 May 2025Merz becomes federal chancellor.

Yellow dots are Merz events. Note how little the two tracks touch: one overlap of about two weeks in March 2020.

Pfizer to BioNTech

On 9 April 2020, Pfizer said it was paying BioNTech $185 million upfront, of which about $113 million was an equity investment, with up to $563 million more in potential milestone payments. Development costs would eventually be split 50/50, but Pfizer would front 100% of them during development and BioNTech would repay its half later. F-01 · F-02 · F-03 · A That's straight from Pfizer's own contemporaneous release.

Brussels to BioNTech

On 11 June 2020, the EIB signed an agreement for up to €100 million in debt financing for the BNT162 program and manufacturing expansion, paid in two €50 million tranches tied to milestones. F-04 · A That's a European public bank, documented on its own website.

Berlin to BioNTech

On 15 September 2020, BioNTech announced up to €375 million from the federal research ministry (BMBF), paid against eight milestones covering trials, production scale-up and authorization. The same release says Pfizer funded its own share without any government money. F-05 · A

And the public paid for the rest

Zoom out and the picture gets even less flattering for the "Wall Street built the vaccine" crowd. A 2023 BMJ study found US public investment in mRNA COVID-19 vaccines reached at least $31.9 billion. A JAMA Network Open study found public sources funded most registered COVID-19 phase 1–3 trials started in 2020–2021, ahead of industry. Before the pandemic, BioNTech's largest owners were the Strüngmann brothers' family office, not an index fund.

The vaccine was built with company cash and a mountain of public money. The asset manager owned tickets to the concert. It didn't write the music.

The trial itself has a sponsor, and it's on the FDA's paperwork: Pfizer and BioNTech. The December 2020 FDA advisory briefing describes a randomized, placebo-controlled phase 2/3 study with about 44,000 participants. BlackRock appears nowhere in the sponsor or protocol documents. T-01 · A T-02 · C

This proves

  • Pfizer fronted development costs and invested ~$113m equity in BioNTech.
  • The EIB lent up to €100m; Germany granted up to ~€375m.
  • Pfizer and BioNTech sponsored the trial.

This does not prove

  • Any BlackRock financing of BNT162.
  • Any role for Merz in any of these deals.
  • Anything at all from the March 2020 calendar overlap.

Remaining gap: the original BMBF grant decision and the EIB disbursement file (milestones → payment dates → work packages) have not been retrieved.

03

The 13F illusion

Why "BlackRock held Pfizer" means less than you think

Here's where the network-diagram crowd really loses the plot. Somebody pulls up a holdings chart, sees BlackRock near the top of Pfizer's shareholder list, sees Merz's name next to BlackRock, and connects the dots. Case closed, right?

Wrong. Let's read the actual document.

A Form 13F is a quarterly filing big US investment managers make to the SEC, listing equity positions they report. BlackRock's 2020 combination report names BlackRock Asset Management Deutschland AG in Munich among its included managers. EC-07 · A So far, so connected.

But the same filing says, in plain text, that BlackRock, Inc. is a parent holding company that does not exercise investment discretion over positions managed by its operating subsidiaries. It reports them because it has a filing obligation, not because it chose them. EC-08 · A

The 13F filing itself says the parent didn't pick the stocks. Anyone still waving that filing as proof Merz ran the vaccine program either can't read or doesn't want to.

Structured third-party pages presenting the 13F data show these positions. They need checking against the underlying SEC rows before anyone uses them for legal or ownership claims. EC-11 · A (as reported)

Different dates. Don't add them up or treat them as one snapshot. Source rows: PortfolioSavvy presentation of SEC 13F data; primary filings still to be inspected.

Look at those numbers for a second. A $15 billion Pfizer position is what you get when you run a huge chunk of the world's index funds and Pfizer is one of the biggest companies in the index. That's ordinary portfolio exposure, and it's the first alternative explanation any honest investigator has to rule out. The BioNTech position, about $54 million at year-end 2020, is a rounding error for a firm that size.

Five separate claims pretending to be one

The meme sells this as one fact. It's actually five, and every one needs its own document:

  1. A BlackRock fund held Pfizer or BioNTech shares Reported
  2. Merz chose those shares No document
  3. The money went to Pfizer or BioNTech No document
  4. That money paid for a specific trial No document
  5. Merz controlled the trial No document

Link three is where it breaks hardest. When a fund buys shares on the stock exchange, the money goes to whoever sold the shares, not to the company. To show BlackRock financed research, you'd need a primary share issue, a loan, an underwriting mandate or a project contract. For BNT162, nobody has found one. F-06 · C H-02 · C

This proves

  • BlackRock's German entity was part of the group's 13F reporting.
  • BlackRock-reported positions existed in Pfizer and BioNTech at the stated dates.

This does not prove

  • Who chose the positions, or why.
  • That BlackRock's own capital owned them.
  • That any of it funded research, or that Merz touched it.

Remaining gap: a quarter-by-quarter 2016–2021 reconstruction from EDGAR 13F, N-PORT and fund reports, classified as passive index, active selection, institutional mandate or not established. Until then, any big "BlackRock owns X" number confuses more than it reveals.

04

Access is not capture

The meetings, and the records nobody had to keep

Now we get to the part that should actually piss you off.

In January 2019, Der Spiegel, citing a dpa report on the federal government's answer to a question from Die Linke MP Fabio De Masi (Bundestag document 19/7190), reported that Merz had met senior federal officials four times in his BlackRock capacity. EC-09 · A

26 Apr 2018Helge Braun, head of the ChancelleryOn the margins of a CDU Economic Council presidium meeting
12 Sep 2018Finance Minister Olaf Scholz and State Secretary Jörg Kukies"Financial-market issues"

A later government answer, Bundestag document 19/18436 of 27 March 2020, adds more: a Konrad-Adenauer-Stiftung discussion with Peter Altmaier on 12 December 2018 on "general matters," a phone call with Kukies on 21 January 2020, and a Merz–Kukies discussion of "current financial-market issues" on 20 March 2020. None lists BioNTech, Pfizer or any pharma subject.

The same document contains the most damning line in this whole story, and it's the government's own: a complete record of such contacts does not exist. Contacts at events, conversations on the sidelines and other informal exchanges were generally not recorded, so the list is "possibly incomplete." It also shows the door stayed open at the very top: BlackRock CEO Larry Fink met State Secretary Kukies on 11 April 2019 and spoke by phone with Finance Minister Scholz on 6 February 2020, both on "current financial-market issues." EC-13 · A

"General matters." "Financial-market issues." That's the whole public record of what the world's biggest asset manager's man in Germany discussed with the people running the country. Not minutes. Not memos. A topic line you could write on a napkin.

The scandal isn't a secret vaccine plot. It's that the system was built so nobody had to write anything down.

The register that came too late

Germany's federal Lobbying Register Act is dated 16 April 2021 and took effect on 1 January 2022. BlackRock Asset Management Deutschland AG's first register entry is dated 14 March 2022. That's nearly two years after Merz left. EC-10 · contradicted as a timing claim So the register can't tell you what he did, and its silence about 2016–2020 proves nothing in either direction. The EU Transparency Register only became de facto mandatory for direct contacts in 2021. And the register is busy now: for 2025, Merz's first year as chancellor, BlackRock Germany declared €770,001–780,000 in lobbying spend, in areas that include pensions, banking and EU legislation.

For the years that matter, the rules didn't require the paper trail you'd need. That's the real indictment. Not of one man, but of a political culture that let a former party leader work the halls for a financial giant and called a one-line topic summary transparency.

Red-teaming our own anger

Now the cold water, because we promised. A meeting is not a decision. A topic line is not a policy change. No document shows any official adopted a BlackRock position or changed a decision because of Merz. M-06 · C EC-12 · unsupported One research memo in the dossier went looking for a link between his BlackRock role and German pharma policy consultations specifically, and found none. The only thing it found was his much earlier, well-known appearance at an employer-backed liberalization event in 2003.

This proves

  • Merz had repeated, high-level government access in his BlackRock capacity.
  • The public record of what was said is nearly empty.
  • The lobbying register didn't exist yet.

This does not prove

  • What was actually said.
  • That any policy changed because of it.
  • Any pharma or vaccine topic.

Remaining gap: the underlying Bundestag question and answer, plus ministry calendars, visitor logs, briefing papers and correspondence from 1 March 2016 to 31 March 2020. That's an IFG request away. See chapter 08.

05

The ugliest rumor

We checked it so you don't have to repeat it

Somewhere in this ecosystem there's a story that an anonymous witness says Merz was involved in "experimenting on people" together with BioNTech's founders, Uğur Şahin and Özlem Türeci. We ran it down.

There's no original statement. No recording. No date, no place, no named victim, no protocol, no document. Nothing.

What the record does show is public, and frankly mundane. As chancellor, Merz gave the laudatory speech when Şahin and Türeci received the German National Prize in Berlin on 10 June 2025. On 9 December 2025 he visited the TRON research institute in Mainz, which they co-founded. That's a politician honoring famous scientists and backing biotech. Every head of government does it.

Meanwhile, the trial framework he'd supposedly been running is documented in the opposite direction. The PEI authorized the German trial after a benefit–risk review and requires a favorable ethics-committee opinion. The trial listings require signed informed consent before any trial procedure. That isn't proof that every consent in every site was perfect. It is a paper trail, and the rumor has none.

One detail is worth checking rather than shouting about: the PEI says it completed the authorization within four days, crediting extensive scientific advice it had given BioNTech beforehand. Fast isn't the same as fraudulent. The way to test it is the review file, not a vibe.

The dossier's verdict: 95% confidence that the allegation is unsubstantiated by any public evidence. That isn't a claim that nothing bad ever happened anywhere. It's a statement that this specific accusation has no support at all. no corroboration

An anonymous accusation of medical crimes against a named human being, with zero particulars, isn't a lead. It's a liability.

Remaining gap: if the witness is real, everything starts with the original material: full statement, provenance, date, specific intervention, location, alleged victim and how the witness knows. Without those, the claim can't be tested, and it shouldn't be printed as anything but unverified.

06

The biological claim

"Something was put into my DNA." How you'd know.

This is the part people feel in their bodies, so it deserves the most respect and the hardest standard. The claim, in its sharpest form: a person's DNA was altered without consent, maybe with non-human genetic material, maybe as part of some program.

Here's the thing almost nobody says out loud. This is one of the most testable claims in this whole story. A meeting can vanish from the record. A conversation can go unminuted. But DNA is a text of about three billion letters. If someone pasted a foreign chapter into it, you can, in principle, point to the letters, the seam where human sequence runs into foreign sequence, and the exact place on the chromosome.

So far, no case-specific evidence of that kind has been supplied in any of the dossiers. No sequencing result, no pathology report, no treatment record, no trial ID, no batch number, no vector sequence, no integration site. B-01 · C That doesn't mean unexplained symptoms aren't real or don't deserve a doctor. It means the hypothesis sits at C until there's material to test.

What the science says, without the spin

Human genetic modification is real. Casgevy, a CRISPR therapy that edits a patient's own blood stem cells outside the body, got EU authorization on 9 February 2024. Human–animal chimeric embryos have been made in labs, published in Cell in 2017, with primate work following in 2021. None of this is science fiction. A

But it's all done in the open, with consent forms, ethics boards, registries and published papers. And a chimera means different cells living side by side, not animal genes spliced into human chromosomes. Words matter here.

On mRNA vaccines specifically: the EMA describes Comirnaty's mRNA as giving cells temporary instructions and then breaking down. One 2022 lab study (Aldén et al.) found vaccine-derived DNA inside a human liver cell line in a dish, and stated plainly that whether it integrated into the genome was unknown. Major reviews say mRNA works without entering the nucleus. "A product contains genetic material" isn't the same as "the recipient's genome was changed." The second claim needs direct genomic evidence. Nothing else counts.

The traps

One sequencing read that looks like an animal, a bacterium or a virus proves nothing. Labs are full of non-human DNA. Your body is full of microbes. Sequencers produce artifacts. One known artifact on some Illumina machines even mimics low-level mutations. A single weird read is where an investigation starts, not where it ends. B-02 · D Symptoms alone can't prove DNA splicing. And even a real, confirmed finding wouldn't identify who did it. B-03 · D

Remaining gap: everything. A medical record of the event in question, a biologically sensible tissue, sequencing, a host–foreign junction, orthogonal validation and a second independent lab. Chapter 08 has the full protocol.

07

The archive of real horrors

What actual non-consensual programs look like when the files open

Anyone who tells you "institutions would never do that" is lying or ignorant. They did. Repeatedly. And we know exactly how we know.

Tuskegee, 1932–1972. The US Public Health Service watched Black men with syphilis go untreated for forty years, without informed consent, and withheld effective treatment once penicillin existed. The CDC documents it on its own website.

Guatemala, 1946–1948. US-funded researchers deliberately infected prisoners, soldiers and psychiatric patients with sexually transmitted diseases without consent. A different, more active kind of abuse than Tuskegee.

The radiation experiments. US official archives document patients injected with plutonium or uranium without convincing evidence they knew they were experimental subjects. In one piece of correspondence, someone recommended keeping certain plutonium data out of hospital records.

MKUltra. The CIA's mind-control program. Director Richard Helms ordered the files destroyed in 1973. The Senate held a hearing in 1977 anyway, because roughly 20,000 pages of financial and administrative records survived in the wrong filing cabinet.

Power will do monstrous things. That's exactly why accusations need evidence: the real monsters were caught with paper.

Look at how every one of these was proven. Not by a movie that resembled it. Not by a feeling. By protocols, participant lists, funding records, correspondence, testimony and official investigations. Even MKUltra, where they shredded on purpose, was cracked by documents that survived the shredding.

And the payoff from those scandals was real: the National Research Act, the Belmont Report, ethics committees and informed consent as law. The machinery we now use to check claims like the ones in this edition exists because people dragged those files into the light.

So history cuts both ways. It kills "they'd never." It also kills "Tuskegee happened, so my theory is proven." Neither follows. The right lesson fits on a bumper sticker: power deserves scrutiny, and accusations deserve evidence.

The same goes for culture. Frankenstein, Doctor Moreau, Jurassic Park, Gattaca, BioShock: great stories, useful metaphors, sometimes good sources of questions. They're not patient files, sequencing data or funding contracts. C-01 · E

Remaining gap: for the present-day claims in this edition, there is no equivalent primary material yet. No protocol, no participant list, no funding record, no testimony on the record.

08

What would actually prove it

A field manual for anyone who wants to close the gaps

Enough about what's missing. Here's how to go get it. None of this needs a hacker or a leak. It needs letters, forms and stubbornness.

Track one: the institutional chain

To prove influence, you need six links, each on paper: (1) the actual fund and manager holding a security; (2) the mandate and who had trading or voting authority; (3) communications or instructions involving a named person; (4) the specific research, regulatory, policy or procurement decision; (5) the responsible institution and decision-maker; and (6) evidence from the time that the person's conduct changed the decision. Break one link and the claim weakens. Find all six and you have a story nobody can kill.

Template · German freedom-of-information (IFG) request Send to the Bundeskanzleramt, the Finance Ministry and the Economy Ministry separately. Narrow beats broad. "Give me everything on BlackRock" goes nowhere. 
All agenda entries, visitor registrations, meeting notes and correspondence between 1 March 2016 and 31 March 2020 in which Friedrich Merz appears in his capacity for BlackRock Asset Management Deutschland AG.

Then: the Handelsregister and Bundesanzeiger for the board structure and annual reports; EDGAR for every 13F, N-PORT and 13G filing from 2016 to 2021; historical EU Transparency Register data and commissioner agendas (DG FISMA, DG COMP, DG SANTE); and the EIB's project register for the BioNTech loan.

Track two: the biological chain

For anyone who believes this happened to them, the order matters more than the budget. Start with the record, not a consumer DNA kit.

DetectLocateValidateReplicateCauseAttribute
  1. Get your full medical record for the event in question: medication and administration logs with batch numbers, consent forms, operation and anesthesia reports, pathology, any trial ID. Ask for documents, not confessions. Step one
  2. See a clinical geneticist at a recognized genetics center. In Belgium, UZ Leuven's Center for Human Genetics is one of eight recognized centers, ISO 15189 accredited. Ask what tissue would be informative. Step two
  3. Short-read whole-genome sequencing for a broad look; long-read sequencing to span structural changes and find a host–foreign junction. Lab
  4. Confirm any candidate with junction-PCR, Sanger sequencing and ddPCR. One technique proves nothing on its own. Validate
  5. A second, independent lab working from its own separately sealed sample, with chain of custody logged. Replicate
  6. Only then: link the signature to a product, batch, date, place and person, and show consent was missing. Attribute

A strong claim reads like this: "In tissue X there is DNA sequence Y, outside normal human variation, physically joined to chromosome Z at junction J, reproduced by two independent labs." That's falsifiable. "I've been hybridized" isn't. One version gets taken seriously in a courtroom. The other gets you laughed out of one.

Belgian routes if records are refused: patient-rights channels through FOD Volksgezondheid; the FAGG for suspected trials and adverse events, using its specific reporting forms (general complaints older than a year aren't handled through the standard route); and the Data Protection Authority (GBA) if health data is withheld. Keep originals: the EML files, letters and receipts, not just copied text.

Remaining gap: this chapter is the gap list. Every step above is a document or a test that hasn't been done yet.

09

The chain is incomplete

Closing argument

So where does that leave us? With two tracks, and we won't blur them for a cheap ending.

The institutional track stands. Friedrich Merz, now Germany's chancellor, spent four years as the chair and door-opener of the world's largest asset manager in Germany. He met a vice chancellor, the head of the Chancellery and the finance minister in that capacity. The public record of what they discussed amounts to a few topic lines. The lobbying register that might have caught it arrived two years after he left. All of that is grade A, and all of it is a legitimate scandal about how money talks to power in Berlin.

The biological track doesn't stand, yet. There is no document connecting Merz or BlackRock to any vaccine decision, trial or financing. There is no sample, sequence or record showing any person's DNA was altered without consent. Those claims sit at C. They might move. They'll move with paper and sequence data, not volume.

Two real networks. No proven bridge. That's not a cover-up and it's not an acquittal. It's the job nobody has done yet.

If you came here wanting the dots connected, sorry. The dots don't connect themselves, and anyone who does it for you without documents is selling something. If you came here wanting to know what's real, here it is: the access was real, the opacity was real, the money trail was public, and the missing links have names, addresses and filing numbers. Go get them.
